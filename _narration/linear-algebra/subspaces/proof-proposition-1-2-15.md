Proof. Suppose u plus W equals v plus W. Since u belongs to u plus W, we have u belongs to v plus W. Hence there exists w in W such that u equals v plus w. It follows that u minus v equals w, which belongs to W. Conversely, suppose u minus v belongs to W. If x belongs to u plus W, then there exists w in W such that x equals u plus w. It follows that x minus v equals the quantity u plus w, minus v, which equals the quantity u minus v, plus w, which belongs to W. Hence x belongs to v plus W. In the same way, if x belongs to v plus W then x belongs to u plus W. Consequently, u plus W equals v plus W. We now suppose x belongs to the intersection of u plus W and v plus W. Then x minus u belongs to W and x minus v belongs to W, which implies u minus v belongs to W, so u plus W equals v plus W. Consequently, if two cosets are not equal, then they are disjoint. This completes the proof.
