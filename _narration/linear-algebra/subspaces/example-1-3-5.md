Example 1.3.5. Let C of R to R denote the set of all continuous real-valued functions defined on R. Since constant functions are continuous functions, zero belongs to C of R to R. Let f and g belong to C of R to R. Since f plus g is continuous function, f plus g belongs to C of R to R. Let f belong to C of R to R and c belong to R. Since c f is a continuous function, c f belongs to C of R to R. Theorem 1.3.2 shows C of R to R is a subspace of R to the R.
