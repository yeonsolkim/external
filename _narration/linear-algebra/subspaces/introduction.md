Within a vector space, we often focus on elements that satisfy a particular condition. The important question is whether the condition survives the operations of the larger space: do addition and scalar multiplication keep us among elements satisfying that condition? If they do, and the zero vector also satisfies the condition, the collection forms a vector space on its own. Such a collection is called a subspace.
