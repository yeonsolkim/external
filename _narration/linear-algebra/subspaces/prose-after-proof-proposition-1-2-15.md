The cosets therefore partition V: each vector belongs to one coset, and two vectors lie in the same coset precisely when their difference belongs to W. If we regard changes by elements of W as insignificant, each coset represents one collection of vectors that we consider equivalent. In particular, all vectors of W belong to the coset W equals zero vector plus W, which will serve as zero. To retain addition and scalar multiplication, we define operations on cosets using representatives and then check that the results do not depend on which representatives we choose.
