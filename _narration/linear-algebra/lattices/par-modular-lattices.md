Modular lattices. The lattices arising from algebraic structures often satisfy additional laws that do not hold in arbitrary lattices. An important example is the lattice of submodules of a module, in which join is given by submodule sum and meet by intersection. Whenever X is contained in Z, these operations satisfy, X plus the quantity Y intersect Z, equals the quantity X plus Y, intersect Z. This identity expresses a compatibility between forming sums of submodules and restricting them by intersection. Its validity follows from the additive structure of modules, but it can be expressed entirely in terms of joins, meets, and the underlying order. We can therefore abstract this property without referring to modules themselves. In an arbitrary lattice, the corresponding left-hand side is always bounded above by the right-hand side; requiring equality gives the modular law. As we shall see, this condition has important structural consequences, including natural isomorphisms between certain intervals.
