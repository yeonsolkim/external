Proposition 1.4.3 shows that a finite sum can be decomposed into two sums over disjoint subsets of its index set. To generalize this to any finite number of groups, we may use a function to assign each index to a group: The fibers of the function determine a decomposition of the index set. The next theorem shows that summing within each group and then summing the resulting partial sums gives the original sum, independently of how the indices are grouped. It is a general principle underlying several fundamental properties of finite sums.
