Example 1.1.3. An m by n matrix with entries from a field F is a rectangular array of the form the matrix with first row a one one, a one two, and so on, up to a one n; second row a two one, a two two, and so on, up to a two n; and so on, down to last row a m one, a m two, and so on, up to a m n, where each a i j is an element of F, called an entry of the matrix. We call the entries a i j with i equals j the diagonal entries. The diagonal entries compose the main diagonal of the matrix. If every entry equals zero, the matrix is called the zero matrix, denoted by capital O. The entries a i one, a i two, and so on, up to a i n compose the i-th row of the matrix, and the entries a one j, a two j, and so on, up to a m j compose the j-th column of the matrix. If the number of rows and columns are equal, the matrix is called a square matrix. The set of all m by n matrices from F is denoted by mat m by n of F. We give mat m by n of F the following operations of matrix addition and scalar multiplication. For A and B in mat m by n of F and c in F, the i j entry of A plus B equals the i j entry of A plus the i j entry of B, and the i j entry of c A equals c times the i j entry of A, for one less than or equal to i less than or equal to m and one less than or equal to j less than or equal to n. Then mat m by n of F is a vector space over F.
