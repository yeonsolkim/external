Financial assets. Financial assets are a class of instruments that represent financial claims on issuers. They also include bank deposits and loans receivable. Within this class, securities, also called issues, are standardized financial claims issued in a form that can be transferred and traded. New issues trade in primary markets when issuers first create and sell them. Subsequent trading takes place in secondary markets, where these instruments are known as seasoned issues. For example, primary trading in new equity issues takes place in the initial public offering, I P O, market. The two main subclasses of securities are equity securities and debt securities: First, equity securities: These represent ownership claims on a corporation or business. The most typical example is common stock. When a company performs well, shareholders may earn substantial returns through dividends and share-price appreciation. If the company goes bankrupt, however, debt holders are repaid first, and equity holders receive only the remaining residual value. Exchange-traded funds, E T Fs, are mutual funds that trade on exchanges. Most E T Fs are index funds. Second, debt securities: These represent contractual claims on an issuer for repayment. A typical example is a bond issued by a corporation. If a company issues a bond with a face value of one thousand dollars, for example, the bondholder becomes a creditor who has lent money to the company.
