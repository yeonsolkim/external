Proof. If no point of K is a limit point of A, then every x in K would have an open ball B of radius r x about x that contains at most one point of A. These balls form an open cover of K. Since K is compact, there exists x one through x n such that K is contained in the union, from i equals one to n, of the ball of radius r sub x i about x i.

Since A is contained in K and each ball contains at most one point of A, the set A has at most n points. This contradicts the assumption that A is infinite. This completes the proof.
