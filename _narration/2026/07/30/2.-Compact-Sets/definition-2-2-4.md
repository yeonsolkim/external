Definition 2.2.4. Let M be a metric space and let A be a subset of N, and N a subset of M. Then, A is compact if it is compact in N. Equivalently, A is compact if every cover of A whose elements are open in N has a finite subcover. In particular, a metric space M is said to be compact if it is compact as a subset of itself.
