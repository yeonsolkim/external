Theorem 2.2.7. Let M be a metric space and let K be contained in M. If K is compact, then every closed subset of K is compact.

Proof. Let F be a closed subset of K, and let the cover U equal the family U i, for i in I, be an open cover of F. Let V equal U union the set containing F complement. Since each U i and F complement are open, V is an open cover of K. Since K is compact, there is a finite subcollection W of V which covers K. Then W covers F. If F complement is a member of W, we may just need to remove it from W, and W set minus the set containing F complement is still an open cover of F. Hence F is compact. This completes the proof.
