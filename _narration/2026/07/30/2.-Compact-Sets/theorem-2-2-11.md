Theorem 2.2.11. Let M be a metric space. If the family F i, for i in I, is a collection of closed subsets of M satisfying the F I P, and F sub i naught is compact for some i naught in I, then the intersection over i in I of F i is nonempty.

Proof. Define K i equals F i intersect F sub i naught for each i in I. Then, we have the intersection from k equals one to n of K sub i k equals F sub i one intersect, and so on up to, F sub i n, intersect F sub i naught, for any finitely many indices i one through i n in I. Since the family F i, for i in I, has the F I P, the right-hand side is nonempty. Therefore the family K i, for i in I, has the F I P. Since F sub i naught is compact and K i is closed in F sub i naught, Theorem 2.2.10 now gives the intersection over i in I of K i is not equal to the empty set. Since the intersection over i in I of K i equals the intersection over i in I of F i, intersect F sub i naught, which equals the intersection over i in I of F i, we finally have the intersection over i in I of F i is not equal to the empty set. This completes the proof.
