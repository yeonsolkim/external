Then, the family of balls of radius n about p, for n in the positive integers, is an open cover of A, and a finite subcover places A inside the ball of radius n zero about p for sufficiently large n zero in N. It follows that the norm of x minus y is less than two n zero for all x and y in A. Thus A is bounded. Also, it is clear that A is closed, by Theorem 2.2.5. Conversely, suppose that A is closed and bounded, and let the point x, equal to x one through x k, be in A. Since A is bounded, for each i, there exists a closed interval from a i to b i that contains every x i. Thus, we have A is contained in I for some k cell I.
