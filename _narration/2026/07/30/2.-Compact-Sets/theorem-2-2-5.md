Theorem 2.2.5. Let M be a metric space and let K be contained in M. If K is compact then K is closed.

Proof. Suppose x belongs to K complement. For y in K, since x is not equal to y, we have d of x, y is greater than zero. Let zero be less than r y, which is less than one half d of x, y.

Then the collection U equals the set of the ball of radius r y about y, such that y belongs to K, is an open cover of K. Since K is compact, there are finitely many points y one through y n in K such that K is contained in the union from i equals one to n of the ball of radius r sub y i about y i, which equals the set U.

Since r y is less than one half d of x, y, we have the ball of radius r sub y i about y i, intersected with the ball of radius r sub y i about x, equals the empty set, for each i equals one through n. Therefore, if r equals the minimum of r sub y one through r sub y n, then the ball of radius r about x does not intersect the set U. Since K is contained in U, we have K intersected with the ball of radius r about x equals the empty set. It follows that the ball of radius r about x is contained in K complement, and thus K complement is open. By Theorem 2.1.14, K is closed. This completes the proof.
