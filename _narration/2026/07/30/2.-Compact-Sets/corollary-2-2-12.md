Corollary 2.2.12. Let M be a metric space. If the sequence K n is a sequence of nonempty compact subsets of M such that K n plus one is contained in K n for n equals one, two, three, and so on, then the intersection from n equals one to infinity of K n is nonempty.

Proof. Let the set K sub n one through K sub n k be a finite subcollection, and put N equals the maximum of n one through n k.

Since K n plus one is contained in K n, we have the intersection from j equals one to k of K sub n j equals K N, which is not equal to the empty set.

Hence the family K n, for n in positive integers, is a collection of closed sets satisfying the F I P. Since K one is compact, Theorem 2.2.11 now gives the intersection from n equals one to infinity of K n equals the intersection over n in positive integers of K n, which is not equal to the empty set. This completes the proof.
