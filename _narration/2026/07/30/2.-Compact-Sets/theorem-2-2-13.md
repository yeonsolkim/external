Theorem 2.2.13, the Nested intervals theorem. If the sequence I n is a sequence of bounded closed intervals in R such that I n plus one is contained in I n, then the intersection from n equals one to infinity of I n is nonempty.

Proof. Let I n equal the closed interval from a n to b n, and let A be the set of all a n. Then, A is nonempty and bounded above by b one. Let alpha be the supremum for A. If m and n belong to the positive integers then we have

a n is less than or equal to a sub m plus n, which is less than b sub m plus n, which is less than or equal to b m,

so that b m is an upper bound for A. Therefore a m is less than or equal to alpha, which is less than or equal to b m for every m, so that alpha belongs to the intersection from n equals one to infinity of I n. This completes the proof.
