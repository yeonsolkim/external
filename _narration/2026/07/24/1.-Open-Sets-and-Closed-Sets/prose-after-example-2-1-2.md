The usual properties of the Euclidean norm imply that d satisfies the metric axioms, so R k, d, is a metric space. Moreover, if A is contained in R k, then the restriction d to A times A, from A times A to R, is again a metric. Hence every subset of Euclidean space becomes a metric space when equipped with the restricted Euclidean metric. More generally, if M, d, is any metric space and N is contained in M, then the restricted metric is a metric on N, called the restricted metric. With the restricted metric, N thus can be regarded as a metric space on its own right.

What we are primarily interested in is the behavior of all points in the distance neighborhood of a given point. Therefore, we consider a set that refers to all points within a distance r from a point in a space.
