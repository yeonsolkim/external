Remark 2.1.20. An important asymmetry is that, in general, the interior of A in N is not equal to the interior of A in M intersect N.

For instance, if M equals R, N equals the closed interval from zero to one, and A equals the interval from zero to one half, closed at zero and open at one half, then zero belongs to the interior of A in N, even though zero does not belong to the interior of A in M. Thus shrinking the ambient space may create new interior points, while closure in the smaller space is obtained simply by intersecting the ambient closure with that space.
