Definition 2.1.6. Let M be a metric space and let A be a subset of M, and let x be in M. x is a closure point of A in M if the intersection of the open ball of radius r about x with A is not equal to the empty set, for all r greater than zero. In words, no matter how tightly we localize around x, x can never get completely away from A. The closure of A in M is the set of all closure points of A, denoted by the closure of A. This includes every point x in A automatically. A limit point deliberately removes this trivial possibility by puncturing the ball: x is a limit point of A in M if the intersection of the punctured open ball of radius r about x with A is not equal to the empty set, for all r greater than zero. The derived set of A in M is the set of all limit points of A, denoted by A prime.
