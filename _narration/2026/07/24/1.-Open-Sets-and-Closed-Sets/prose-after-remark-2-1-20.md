For instance, if M equals R, N equals the closed interval from zero to one, and A equals the interval from zero to one half, including zero and excluding one half, then zero belongs to the interior of A in N, even though zero does not belong to the interior of A in M. Thus shrinking the ambient space may create new interior points, while closure in the smaller space is obtained simply by intersecting the ambient closure with that space.
