Theorem 2.1.19. Let M be a metric space, and regard N contained in M as a metric space with the restricted metric. Then, for a set A contained in N, the closure of A in N equals the closure of A in M intersect N.

Proof. If x belongs to the closure of A in N, then every open ball B r in N of x meets A. Thus B r in M of x meets A. Hence x belongs to the closure of A in M, and thus x belongs to the closure of A in M intersect N. If x belongs to the closure of A in M intersect N, then every open ball B r in M of x meets A. Since A is contained in N, the intersection point must be in N. Thus, even if we take the intersection of B r in M of x with N, it still meets A; that is, B r in N of x meets A. Therefore x belongs to the closure of A in N. This completes the proof.
