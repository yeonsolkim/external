Definition 2.1.1. Suppose that M is a nonempty set. A function d from M squared to the real numbers is called a metric on M if, for all x, y, and z in M, Property one: zero is less than or equal to d of x and y. Property two: d of x and y equals zero if and only if x equals y. Property three: d of x and y equals d of y and x. Property four: d of x and z is less than or equal to d of x and y plus d of y and z. Then, the set M equipped with the metric d is called a metric space.
