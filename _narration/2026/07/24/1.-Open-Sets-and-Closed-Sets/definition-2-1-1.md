Definition 2.1.1. Suppose that M is a nonempty set. A function d from M squared to R is called a metric on M if, for all x, y, z in M,

Property one: zero is less than or equal to d of x, y.

Property two: d of x, y equals zero if and only if x equals y.

Property three: d of x, y equals d of y, x.

Property four: d of x, z is less than or equal to d of x, y plus d of y, z.

Then, the set M equipped with the metric d is called a metric space.
