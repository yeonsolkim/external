The closure of A in M is the set of all closure points of A, denoted by the closure of A. This includes every point x in A automatically. A limit point deliberately removes this trivial possibility by puncturing the ball: x is a limit point of A in M if the punctured open ball of radius r about x intersect A is not equal to the empty set, for all r greater than zero. The derived set of A in M is the set of all limit points of A, denoted by A prime.
