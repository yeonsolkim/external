Definition 2.1.4. Let M be a metric space and let A be contained in M. x belongs to M is an interior point of A in M if the open ball of radius r about x is contained in A, for some r greater than zero. Conversely, x belongs to M is an exterior point of A in M if the open ball of radius r about x is contained in the complement of A, for some r greater than zero. The remaining possibility is that no ball centered at x lies entirely in A or entirely in the complement of A. That is, if the intersection of the open ball of radius r about x and A is not equal to the empty set, and the intersection of the open ball of radius r about x and the complement of A is not equal to the empty set, for all r greater than zero, then x is called a boundary point of A in M. The interior of A denotes the interior of A in M, the set of all interior points of A in M; the exterior of A denotes the exterior of A in M, the set of all exterior points of A in M; the boundary of A denotes the boundary of A in M, the set of all boundary points of A in M.
