Definition 2.1.12. Let M be a metric space and let A be contained in M. A is open in M if every point of A is an interior point of A. Equivalently, A is open in M if and only if the interior of A equals A.

A is closed in M if it contains all of its limit points. Equivalently, A is closed in M if and only if the closure of A equals A.

Since openness was defined in terms of open balls, the first basic fact to verify is that open balls are themselves open.
