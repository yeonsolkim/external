Definition 3.4.2. Let the sequence x n be a sequence in R. For each k, define

s k equals the supremum of the set of x n such that n is greater than or equal to k, and t k equals the infimum of the set of x n such that n is greater than or equal to k.

The upper limit and lower limit of the sequence x n are defined, respectively, by

the limit superior of x n equals the infimum over k greater than or equal to one of s k, and the limit inferior of x n equals the supremum over k greater than or equal to one of t k.

The sequence s k is monotonically decreasing, while the sequence t k is monotonically increasing. Thus s k and t k form, respectively, upper and lower envelopes for the successive tails of the sequence x n. The upper and lower limits record the eventual values of these two envelopes. The tail-based definition also has a subsequential interpretation: the upper and lower limits are precisely the largest and smallest extended limits that can be obtained along subsequences.
