One. Let S be the set of every supremum s k for the k-th tail. Since y is greater than infimum S, y is not a lower bound for S. So there exists k naught such that s sub k naught is less than y. Then y is an upper bound for the k naught-th tail. Since s k is monotonically decreasing, n greater than or equal to k naught implies x n is less than y.

Two. Let T be the set of every infimum t k for the k-th tail. Since y is less than supremum T, y is not an upper bound for T. So there exists k naught such that t sub k naught is greater than y. Then y is a lower bound for the k naught-th tail. Since t k is monotonically increasing, n greater than or equal to k naught implies x n is greater than y.

This completes the proof.
