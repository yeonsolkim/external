Proof. One. Let S be the set of every supremum s k for the k-th tail. Since y is greater than the infimum of S, y is not a lower bound for S. So there exists k naught such that s sub k naught is less than y. Then y is an upper bound for the k naught-th tail. Since the sequence s k is monotonically decreasing, n greater than or equal to k naught implies x n is less than y.

Two. Let T be the set of every infimum t k for the k-th tail. Since y is less than the supremum of T, y is not an upper bound for T. So there exists k naught such that t sub k naught is greater than y. Then y is a lower bound for the k naught-th tail. Since the sequence t k is monotonically increasing, n greater than or equal to k naught implies x n is greater than y. This completes the proof.
