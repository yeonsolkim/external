Example 3.4.4. Let x n be a sequence in R such that the set of x n such that n is greater than or equal to one equals Q. Let y in R be given. Suppose n one is less than, and so on up to, n k minus one have been chosen. Since the set x one through x sub n k minus one is finite and the interval from y minus one over k to y plus one over k has infinitely many rationals, there is a rational q which is not in the set x one through x sub n k minus one but belongs to the interval. Since q appears in x n at least once, there exists n k greater than n k minus one such that x sub n k equals q. In this way, we obtain a subsequence x sub n k that converges to y. Since y was arbitrary, every real number is a subsequential limit. Let A be the set of all extended subsequential limits of x n. Since the supremum of A is greater than or equal to the supremum of R, by Theorem 3.4.3, lim sup of x n equals the supremum of A equals plus infinity.

Similarly, lim inf of x n equals the infimum of A equals minus infinity.
