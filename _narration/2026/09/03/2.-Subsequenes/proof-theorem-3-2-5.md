Proof. Let A be the set of all subsequential limits of the sequence x n, and let y in M be a limit point of A. We show that there exists a subsequence of the sequence x n that converges to y. There is n in the positive integers such that x n is not equal to y. Indeed, if x one equals x two, and so on, and all are equal to y, then A equals the singleton set consisting of y, which contradicts the fact that A is infinite. Choose n one equals n and put delta equals d of x sub n one and y. Suppose n one through n sub k minus one are chosen. Since y is a limit point of A, there is z in A such that d of z and y is less than two to the minus k, times delta. Since z is a subsequential limit of the sequence x n, there exists n k greater than n sub k minus one such that d of x sub n k and z is less than two to the minus k, times delta. Thus d of x sub n k and y is less than or equal to d of x sub n k and z plus d of z and y, which is less than two to the power one minus k, times delta.

Therefore, the subsequence x sub n k converges to y; hence y belongs to A. This completes the proof.
