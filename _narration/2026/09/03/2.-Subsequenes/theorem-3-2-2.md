Theorem 3.2.2. Let M be a metric space. A sequence x n converges to x if and only if every subsequence of x n converges to x.

Proof. Suppose x n converges to x and let x n k be a subsequence of x n. For any epsilon greater than zero, there exists n naught in the positive integers such that n greater than or equal to n naught implies d of x n and x is less than epsilon. Since the set of indices n k, for k in the positive integers, is infinite, there exists k naught in the positive integers such that k naught is greater than or equal to n naught. Hence, k greater than or equal to k naught implies d of x n k and x is less than epsilon; that is, x n k converges to x. Conversely, suppose every subsequence of x n converges to x. Put n k equals k. Then the sequence x n k equals the sequence x k, which equals the sequence x n. Therefore x n converges to x. This completes the proof.
