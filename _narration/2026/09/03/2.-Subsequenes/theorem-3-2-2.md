Theorem 3.2.2. Let M be a metric space. A sequence x n converges to x if and only if every subsequence of x n converges to x.

Proof. Suppose x n tends to x and let x sub n k be a subsequence of x n. For any epsilon greater than zero, there exists n zero in the positive integers such that n greater than or equal to n zero implies d of x n, x, is less than epsilon. Since the set n k, for k greater than or equal to one, is infinite, there exists k zero in the positive integers such that k zero is greater than or equal to n zero. Hence, k greater than or equal to k zero implies d of x sub n k, x, is less than epsilon; that is, x sub n k tends to x. Conversely, suppose every subsequence of x n converges to x. Put n k equal to k. Then x sub n k equals x k, which equals x n. Therefore x n tends to x. This completes the proof.
