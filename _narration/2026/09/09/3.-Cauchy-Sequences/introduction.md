Convergence describes the asymptotic behavior of a sequence by relating its terms to a point of the ambient space. This raises a natural question. How much of this asymptotic behavior can be recognized from the terms of the sequence themselves, without first specifying or establishing the existence of such a point? The concept of Cauchyness isolates precisely this internal aspect of convergence. Instead of comparing the terms x n with a separate point x, it compares sufficiently late terms with one another and asks whether they eventually become arbitrarily close.
