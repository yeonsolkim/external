Definition 3.3.1. Let M be a metric space and let x n be a sequence in M. The sequence x n is called a Cauchy sequence if for any epsilon greater than zero, there exists n naught in the positive integers such that m and n greater than or equal to n naught imply d of x m, x n is less than epsilon.

The Cauchy condition can be viewed geometrically as requiring that the tails of the sequence eventually become arbitrarily small as sets. To make this precise, we introduce the diameter of a set.
