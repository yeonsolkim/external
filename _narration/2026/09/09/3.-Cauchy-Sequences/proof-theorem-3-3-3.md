Proof. Let a k equal the diameter of E k. Suppose the sequence x n is a Cauchy sequence, and let epsilon greater than zero be given. Then, there is n naught in the positive integers such that m and n greater than or equal to n naught implies d of x m and x n is less than epsilon over two. Hence the set of the reals of the form d of x m and x n is bounded above, and the supremum is a sub n naught. Therefore, we have a sub n naught is less than epsilon. Since E sub k plus one is contained in E k, we have a sub k plus one is less than or equal to a k, so zero is less than or equal to a k, which is at most a sub n naught, which is less than epsilon for every k greater than or equal to n naught. Hence a k tends to zero. Conversely, suppose a k tends to zero, and let epsilon greater than zero be given. Then, there is k naught in the positive integers such that a sub k naught is less than epsilon. That is, if m and n are greater than or equal to k naught then d of x m and x n is less than or equal to a sub k naught, which is less than epsilon. Thus the sequence x n is a Cauchy sequence. This completes the proof.
