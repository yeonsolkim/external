Theorem 3.3.3. Let the sequence x n be a sequence in a metric space M, and let E k equal the set of x n such that n is greater than or equal to k. Then, the sequence x n is a Cauchy sequence if and only if the limit as k tends to infinity of the diameter of E k equals zero.

Proof. Let a k equal the diameter of E k. Suppose the sequence x n is a Cauchy sequence, and let epsilon greater than zero be given. Then, there is n naught in the positive integers such that m and n greater than or equal to n naught implies d of x m, x n is less than epsilon over two. Hence the set of the reals of the form d of x m, x n is bounded above, and the supremum is a sub n naught. Therefore, we have a sub n naught is less than epsilon, which implies a k tends to zero. Conversely, suppose a k tends to zero, and let epsilon greater than zero be given. Then, there is k naught in the positive integers such that a sub k naught is less than epsilon. That is, if m and n are greater than or equal to k naught then d of x m, x n is less than a sub k naught, which is less than or equal to epsilon.

Thus the sequence x n is a Cauchy sequence. This completes the proof.
