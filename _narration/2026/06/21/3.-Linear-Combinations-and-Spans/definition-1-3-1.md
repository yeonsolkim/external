Definition 1.3.1. Let V be a vector space over F, and let S be a nonempty subset of V. A vector v in V is called a linear combination of the vectors in S if there exist a finite number of vectors u one through u n in S and scalars a one through a n in F such that v equals a one times u one plus, and so on, up to a n times u n.

In this case, we also say that v is a linear combination of u one through u n, and we call a one through a n the coefficients of the linear combination.
