Proof. Part one. Let the family S i indexed by I be any collection of elements of the collection C. If I equals the empty set, its intersection is X. Since the closure of X is contained in X and extensivity gives the reverse inclusion, the closure of X equals X. Thus the empty intersection belongs to the collection C. Now assume I is not equal to the empty set, and put S equal to the intersection over i in I of S i. For every i in I, we have S is contained in S i. By monotonicity and the fact that S i is closed, the closure of S is contained in the closure of S i, which equals S i. It follows that the closure of S is contained in S. Since closure is extensive, S is contained in the closure of S, so the closure of S equals S. Hence S is closed and belongs to the collection C. Therefore the collection C is closed under arbitrary intersections, including the empty intersection, and is a Moore collection by Definition 1.2.8. This completes the proof.
