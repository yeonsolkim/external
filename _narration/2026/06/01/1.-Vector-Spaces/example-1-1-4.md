Example 1.1.4. Let S be a nonempty set and F be a field, and let F to the S denote the set of all functions from S to F. We give F to the S the operations of point-wise addition and scalar multiplication: For f and g in F to the S and c in F, f plus g, evaluated at x, equals f of x plus g of x, and c f, evaluated at x, equals c times f of x, for all x in S. Two functions f and g are equal if f of x equals g of x for all x in S, and the zero function is defined by the zero function of x equals zero for all x in S. We can see that F to the S is a vector space over F.
