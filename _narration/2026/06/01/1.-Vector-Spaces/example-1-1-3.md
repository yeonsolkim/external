Example 1.1.3. An m by n matrix with entries from a field F is a rectangular array of the form the matrix whose first row is a one one, a one two, and so on, up to a one n, whose second row is a two one, a two two, and so on, up to a two n, and so on, down to the last row, a m one, a m two, and so on, up to a m n, where each a i j is element of F, called an entry of the matrix. We call the entries a i j with i equals j the diagonal entries. The diagonal entries compose the main diagonal of the matrix. If every entry equals zero, the matrix is called the zero matrix, denoted by the zero matrix. The entries a i one, a i two, and so on, up to a i n compose the i-th row of the matrix, and the entries a one j, a two j, and so on, up to a m j compose the j-th column of the matrix. If the number of rows and columns are equal, the matrix is called a square matrix. The set of all m by n matrices from F is denoted by F m by n. We give F m by n the following operations of matrix addition and scalar multiplication. For A and B in F m by n and c in F, the i j entry of A plus B equals the i j entry of A plus the i j entry of B, and the i j entry of c A equals c times the i j entry of A, for one less than or equal to i, which is less than or equal to m, and one less than or equal to j, which is less than or equal to n. Then F m by n is a vector space over F.
