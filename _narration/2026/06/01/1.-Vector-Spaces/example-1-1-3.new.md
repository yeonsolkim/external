Example 1.1.3. An m by n matrix with entries from a field F is a rectangular array of the form the matrix whose first row is a sub one one, a sub one two, and so on, up to a sub one n; whose second row is a sub two one, a sub two two, and so on, up to a sub two n; and so on, down to whose m-th row is a sub m one, a sub m two, and so on, up to a sub m n, where each a sub i j is an element of F, called an entry of the matrix. We call the entries a sub i j with i equals j the diagonal entries. The diagonal entries compose the main diagonal of the matrix. If every entry equals zero, the matrix is called the zero matrix, denoted by capital O. The entries a sub i one, a sub i two, and so on, up to a sub i n compose the i-th row of the matrix, and the entries a sub one j, a sub two j, and so on, up to a sub m j compose the j-th column of the matrix. If the number of rows and columns are equal, the matrix is called a square matrix. The set of all m by n matrices from F is denoted by mat m by n of F. We give mat m by n of F the following operations of matrix addition and scalar multiplication. For A and B in mat m by n of F and c in F, we have A plus B, sub i j, equals A sub i j plus B sub i j, and c A, sub i j, equals c A sub i j, for one less than or equal to i less than or equal to m and one less than or equal to j less than or equal to n. Then mat m by n of F is a vector space over F.
