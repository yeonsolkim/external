Example 1.1.2. Let F be a field. An object of the form the n-tuple a one through a n, with a one, a two, and so on, up to a n in F, is called an n-tuple with entries from F. The set of all n-tuples from F is denoted by F n. We give F n the operations of coordinate-wise addition and scalar multiplication. For u equals the n-tuple a one through a n in F n, v equals the n-tuple b one through b n in F n, and c in F, u plus v equals the n-tuple a one plus b one, a two plus b two, and so on, up to a n plus b n, and c times u equals the n-tuple c a one, c a two, and so on, up to c a n. Then, F n is a vector space over F. A row vector, the n-tuple a one through a n, may be written as a column vector with entries a one, a two, and so on, down to a n.
