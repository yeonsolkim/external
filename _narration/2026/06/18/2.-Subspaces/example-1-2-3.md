Example 1.2.3. Let A be an m by n matrix with entries from a field F. The transpose of A, denoted by A transpose, is the unique matrix whose i-th row is the i-th column of A, for each i from one to n, where a row and a column are identified with the same coordinate tuple. Then, the i j entry of A transpose is the j-th entry of the i-th column of A, namely, A j i. Consequently, one may think of A transpose as being obtained from A by interchanging the rows with the columns. If A transpose equals A, then A is called a symmetric matrix. In this case, A is a square matrix with A i j equals A j i. The set W of all symmetric matrices in n by n matrices over F is a subspace of n by n matrices over F. Indeed, O is in W since O transpose equals O. Let A and B be in W. We have A i j equals A j i and B i j equals B j i, thus the i j entry of the matrix A plus B equals A i j plus B i j, which equals A j i plus B j i, which equals the j i entry of the matrix A plus B. Therefore A plus B is in W. Let A be in W and c be in F. We have A i j equals A j i, thus the i j entry of the matrix c A equals c A i j, which equals c A j i, which equals the j i entry of the matrix c A. Therefore c A is in W.
