Example 1.2.3. Let A be an m by n matrix with entries from a field F. The transpose of A, denoted by A transpose, is the unique matrix whose i-th row is the i-th column of A, for each i equals one through n, where a row and a column are identified with the same coordinate tuple. Then, the i j entry of A transpose is the j-th entry of the i-th column of A, namely, A sub j i. Consequently, one may think of A transpose as being obtained from A interchanging the rows with the columns. If A transpose equals A, then A is called a symmetric matrix. In this case, A is a square matrix with A sub i j equals A sub j i. The set W of all symmetric matrix in F n by n is a subspace of F n by n. Indeed, the zero matrix belongs to W since zero matrix transpose equals zero matrix. Let A and B be in W. We have A sub i j equals A sub j i and B sub i j equals B sub j i, thus the i j entry of A plus B equals A sub i j plus B sub i j, which equals A sub j i plus B sub j i, which equals the j i entry of A plus B. Therefore A plus B belongs to W. Let A be in W and c be in F. We have A sub i j equals A sub j i, thus the i j entry of c A equals c A sub i j, which equals c A sub j i, which equals the j i entry of c A. Therefore c A belongs to W.
