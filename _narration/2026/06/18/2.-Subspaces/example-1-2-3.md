Example 1.2.3. Let A be an m by n matrix with entries from a field F. The transpose of A, denoted by A transpose, is the unique matrix whose i-th row is the i-th column of A, for each i from one to n, where a row and a column are identified with the same coordinate tuple. Then, the i j entry of A transpose is the j-th entry of the i-th column of A, namely, A j i. Consequently, one may think of A transpose as being obtained from A by interchanging the rows with the columns. If A transpose equals A, then A is called a symmetric matrix. In this case, A is a square matrix with A i j equals A j i. The set W of all symmetric matrices in F n by n is a subspace of F n by n. Indeed, the zero matrix belongs to W since zero matrix transpose equals zero matrix. Let A and B be in W. We have A i j equals A j i and B i j equals B j i, thus the i j entry of the sum A plus B equals A i j plus B i j, which equals A j i plus B j i, which equals the j i entry of the sum A plus B. Therefore A plus B belongs to W. Let A belong to W and c belong to F. We have A i j equals A j i, thus the i j entry of c A equals c times A i j, which equals c times A j i, which equals the j i entry of c A. Therefore c A belongs to W.
