Definition 2.3.4, Cantor set. Let C zero be the interval the closed interval from zero to one. Removing the open interval from one third to two thirds from C zero, let C one be the union of the intervals the closed interval from zero to one third, and the closed interval from two thirds to one.

Removing the open middle thirds of the intervals, let C two be the union of the intervals the closed interval from zero to one ninth, the closed interval from two ninths to one third, the closed interval from two thirds to seven ninths, and the closed interval from eight ninths to one.

Continuing in this way, we obtain a sequence of sets C n. Then, the set C defined by C equals the intersection from n equals one to infinity of C n is called the Cantor set.
