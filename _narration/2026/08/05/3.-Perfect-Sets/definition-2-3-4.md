Definition 2.3.4. Cantor set. Let C naught be the interval the closed interval from zero to one. Removing the open interval from one over three to two over three from C naught, let C one be the union of the intervals the closed interval from zero to one over three, and the closed interval from two over three to one.

Removing the open middle thirds of the intervals, let C two be the union of the intervals the closed interval from zero to one over nine, the closed interval from two over nine to one over three, the closed interval from two over three to seven over nine, and the closed interval from eight over nine to one.

Continuing in this way, we obtain a sequence of sets C n. Then, the set C defined by C equals the intersection from n equals one to infinity of C n is called the Cantor set.
