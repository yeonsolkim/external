Remark 2.3.2. As openness and closedness, perfectness may depend on the ambient space. For example, Q has no isolated points and is closed in itself, so it is perfect. In R, however, Q is not perfect in R. Indeed, since Q is a proper subset of R, we may choose x in R setminus Q. Then, x is a limit point of Q, since Theorem 1.1.11 guarantees that every interval from x minus r to x plus r has a rational. Thus Q is not closed in R. Therefore, despite having no isolated points, Q is not perfect in R.
