Definition 3.1.1. Let M be a metric space. A sequence x n of points in M is said to converge in M if there is a point x in M with the following property: For any epsilon greater than zero, there exists n naught in the positive integers such that n greater than or equal to n naught implies d of x n, x, is less than epsilon.

In this case, we also say that x n converges to x, or that x is the limit of x n, and we write x n tends to x, or limit as n goes to infinity of x n equals x. If x n does not converge, it is said to diverge in M.
