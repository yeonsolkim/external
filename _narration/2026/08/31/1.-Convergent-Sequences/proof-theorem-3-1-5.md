Proof. Suppose x n is less than or equal to x n plus one for n equals one, two, three, and so on, and let alpha be the supremum for the set x n, for n in positive integers. Given epsilon greater than zero, by lemma 1.1.10, there is n naught in positive integers such that x sub n naught belongs to the interval from alpha minus epsilon to alpha, open at alpha minus epsilon and closed at alpha. Then, n greater than or equal to n naught implies the absolute value of alpha minus x n is less than epsilon; hence the sequence x n converges to alpha. The converse follows from part three of Theorem 3.1.3. In the other case, where the sequence x n is monotonically decreasing, the proof is analogous. This completes the proof.
