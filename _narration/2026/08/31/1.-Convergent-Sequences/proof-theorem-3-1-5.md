Proof. Suppose x n is less than or equal to x n plus one for n equals one, two, three, and so on, and let alpha be the supremum for the sequence x n, for n in the positive integers. Given epsilon greater than zero, by lemma 1.1.10, there is n zero in the positive integers such that x n zero belongs to the interval from alpha minus epsilon to alpha, open on the left and closed on the right. Then, n greater than or equal to n zero implies the absolute value of alpha minus x n is less than epsilon; hence x n converges to alpha. The converse follows from part three of Theorem 3.1.3. In the other case, where x n is monotonically decreasing, the proof is analogous. This completes the proof.
