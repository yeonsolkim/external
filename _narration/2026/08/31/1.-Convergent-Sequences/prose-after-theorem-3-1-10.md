Taken together, these assertions describe several basic relations among powers, roots, and exponential sequences. Assertion one says that the reciprocal of every fixed positive power of n tends to zero. Assertion two says that taking successively higher roots removes the scale of any fixed positive number: regardless of its original size, its n-th root approaches one. Assertion three shows that the same conclusion remains true even when the quantity under the root varies with n. Thus, although n itself tends to infinity, its growth is not fast enough to produce an n-th root bounded away from one. Assertions four and five describe two sides of exponential behavior. In four, n to the y is a fixed power of n, whereas the quantity one plus x, to the n, is an exponential sequence with fixed base greater than one. Both x and y are held fixed as n goes to infinity. Therefore, no matter how close one plus x is to one, or how large y is, the exponential term eventually dominates the power term. Assertion five gives the corresponding statement for exponential decay: repeated multiplication by a fixed number whose absolute value is less than one forces the resulting sequence to zero.
