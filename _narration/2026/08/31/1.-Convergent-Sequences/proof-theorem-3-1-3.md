Proof.

Part one. Suppose x n goes to x and let the ball of radius r about x be an open ball of x. Corresponding to this r, there exists n zero in the positive integers such that n greater than or equal to n zero implies x n belongs to the ball of radius r about x. Conversely, suppose that every open ball of x contains x n for all but finitely many n. Fix r greater than zero and let n zero be greater than the maximum of the set of positive integers n such that x n does not belong to the ball of radius r about x. Then n greater than or equal to n zero implies x n belongs to the ball of radius r about x. Thus x n goes to x.

Part two. Given epsilon greater than zero, there exist n sub zero x and n sub zero y in the positive integers such that n greater than or equal to n sub zero x implies d of x n and x is less than epsilon over two, and n greater than or equal to n sub zero y implies d of x n and y is less than epsilon over two. If n zero equals the maximum of the set consisting of n sub zero x and n sub zero y, then we have d of x and y is less than or equal to d of x n and x plus d of x n and y, which is less than epsilon. Since epsilon was arbitrary, we conclude that d of x and y equals zero; hence x equals y.

Part three. Suppose x n goes to x. There exists n zero in the positive integers such that n greater than n zero implies x n belongs to the unit ball about x. Put epsilon equal to the maximum of the set consisting of d of x one and x, and so on up to d of x sub n zero and x, plus one. Then we have d of x n and x is less than epsilon for all n in the positive integers; hence x n is bounded.

Part four. For each n in the positive integers, there is a point x n in A such that d of x n and x is less than one over n. Given epsilon greater than zero, choose n zero so that one over n zero is less than or equal to epsilon. Then, n greater than or equal to n zero implies d of x n and x is less than epsilon; hence x n goes to x. This completes the proof.
