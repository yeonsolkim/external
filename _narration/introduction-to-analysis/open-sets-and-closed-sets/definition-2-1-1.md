Definition 2.1.1. Suppose that M is a nonempty set. A function d from M squared to the real numbers is called a metric on M if, for all x, y, and z in M, the following hold.

First, zero is less than or equal to d of x and y.

Second, d of x and y equals zero holds if and only if x equals y.

Third, d of x and y equals d of y and x.

Fourth, d of x and z is less than or equal to d of x and y plus d of y and z.

Then, the set M equipped with the metric d is called a metric space.
