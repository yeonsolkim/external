Proof. Since the empty set is closed, we may assume K is not equal to the empty set. Suppose x belongs to K complement. For y in K, since x is not equal to y, we have, d of x and y, is greater than zero. Let zero be less than r y, which is less than one half times d of x and y. Then the collection U equals the family of balls of radius r y about y, for y in K, is an open cover of K. Since K is compact, there are finitely many points y one through y n in K such that K is contained in the union, from i equals one to n, of the ball of radius r sub y i about y i, which equals U. Since r y is less than one half times d of x and y, we have, the ball of radius r sub y i about y i intersected with the ball of radius r sub y i about x, equals the empty set, for each i from one to n. Therefore, if r equals the minimum of the set consisting of r sub y one through r sub y n, then the ball of radius r about x, does not intersect U. Since K is contained in U, we have, K intersected with the ball of radius r about x, equals the empty set. It follows that, the ball of radius r about x, is contained in K complement, and thus K complement is open. By Theorem 2.1.14, K is closed. This completes the proof.
