**Definition 2.3.1.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq M.$ An *open cover* of $A$ is a family $\mathcal U=\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ of open subsets of $M$ such that

$$
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i.
$$

<br>

**Definition 2.3.2.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq E\subseteq M.$ The set $A$ is *compact relative to* $E$ if every family $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ of subsets open relative to $E$ satisfying

$$
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i
$$

contains a finite subcover of $A$; that is, there exist $i_1,\dots,i_n\in I$ such that

$$
A\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}U_{i_k}.
$$

<br>

**Theorem 2.3.3.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq E\subseteq M.$ Then $A$ is compact relative to $M$ if and only if $A$ is compact relative to $E.$<br><br>
*Proof.* Suppose that $A$ is compact relative to $M.$ Let $\lbrace U_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ be a collection of sets, open relative to $E,$ such that $A\subseteq \bigcup_{i\in I}U_i.$ By Theorem 2.2.22, there are sets $V_i,$ open relative to $M,$ such that $U_i = E\cap V_i,$ for all $i\in I$; and since $A$ is compact relative to $M,$ we have 

$$ A\subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^{n}V_{i_k}\tag{\(\ast\)}$$

for some finitely many indices $i_1,\dots,i_n\in I.$ Since $A\subseteq E,$ $(\ast\ast)$ implies 

$$ A\subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^{n}U_{i_k}.\tag{\(\ast\ast\)}$$

<br>
