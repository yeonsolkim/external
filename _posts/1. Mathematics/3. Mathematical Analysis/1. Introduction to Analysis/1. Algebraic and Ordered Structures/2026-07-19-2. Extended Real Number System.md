**Definition 1.2.1.** The set $\overline{\mathbb R}$ is the set $\mathbb R$ with two distinct sets $-\infty$ and $+\infty$ which do not belong to $\mathbb R.$ Equivalently,

$$\overline{\mathbb R} = \mathbb R \cup \{-\infty, +\infty\}.$$

Then we extend the usual order on $\mathbb R$ to observe the following rule:

$$
\forall x \in \mathbb R\ (-\infty < x < +\infty).
$$

**Remark 1.2.2.** Let $A \subseteq \mathbb R$. Its extended supremum and extended infimum are, respectively,

$$ \sup_{\overline{\mathbb R}}A =
\begin{cases}
\sup A & \text{if } A \text{ is nonempty and bounded above},\\
+\infty & \text{if } A \text{ is unbounded above},\\
-\infty & \text{if } A = \varnothing,
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$ \inf_{\overline{\mathbb R}}A =
\begin{cases}
\inf A & \text{if } A \text{ is nonempty and bounded below},\\
-\infty & \text{if } A \text{ is unbounded below},\\
+\infty & \text{if } A = \varnothing.
\end{cases}
$$

**Definition 1.2.3.** For all $x\in \mathbb R,$

$$x + (+\infty) = +\infty, \qquad x + (-\infty) = -\infty, \qquad \frac{x}{+\infty} = \frac{x}{-\infty}=0.$$

If $0<x$ then

$$x\cdot (+\infty) = +\infty, \qquad x\cdot (-\infty) = -\infty,$$

and, if $x<0$ then

$$x\cdot (+\infty) = -\infty, \qquad x\cdot (-\infty) = +\infty.$$
