**Axiom 2.2.1.** Each axiom represents a scheme, where $\phi,\psi,$ and $\theta$ are formulas, $x$ is a variable, and $t,u,$ and $v$ are terms. All substitutions replace only free occurrences of $x$ and require that the substituted term is free for $x$ in the formula.

1. $\phi \rightarrow(\psi \rightarrow \phi).$
2. $(\phi \rightarrow(\psi \rightarrow \theta)) \rightarrow((\phi \rightarrow \psi) \rightarrow(\phi \rightarrow \theta)).$
3. $((\neg \phi) \rightarrow(\neg \psi)) \rightarrow(\psi \rightarrow \phi).$
{:marker-prefix="P"}

4. $(\forall x) \phi \rightarrow \phi[t / x].$
5. $(\forall x)(\phi \rightarrow \psi) \rightarrow(\phi \rightarrow(\forall x) \psi),$ if there is no free occurrence of $x$ in $\phi.$
{:marker-prefix="Q"}

6. $t=t$.
7. $(t=u) \rightarrow(u=t)$.
8. $(t=u) \rightarrow((u=v) \rightarrow(t=v))$.
9. $(t=u) \rightarrow(\phi[t / x] \rightarrow \phi[u / x]).$
{:marker-prefix="E"}

**Rule 2.2.2 (Rules of inference).** Let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be formulas, and let $x$ be a variable. Let $\Gamma$ be the set of undischarged assumptions.

1. From $\phi$ and $\phi \rightarrow \psi,$ infer $\psi$ (modus ponens).
2. From $\phi$ infer $(\forall x)\phi,$ provided $x$ is not free in any formula of $\Gamma$ (generalization).


**Proposition 2.2.3.** Let $\phi$ be formula, $x$ be a variable, and let $t$ be free term for $x$ in $\phi.$
1. $(\forall x)\phi\rightarrow \phi[t/x]$ (universal instantiation).
2. $\phi[t/x]\rightarrow (\exists x)\phi$ (existential introduction).


**Definition 2.2.4.** Let $\Gamma$ be a set of $\mathcal L$-formulas. A *derivation from* $\Gamma$ is a finite sequence of formulas $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n$ in which each formula is
1. an instance of a logical axiom scheme,
2. a member of $\Gamma,$ or
3. obtained from earlier formulas by a rule of inference, with the restriction on Generalization checked against $\Gamma.$

We write $\Gamma\vdash\phi$ if there is a derivation from $\Gamma$ whose last formula is $\phi.$ We write $\vdash\phi$ when $\Gamma=\varnothing,$ and call $\phi$ a *theorem* of first-order logic.

**Theorem 2.2.5 (Deduction theorem).** Let $T$ be an $\mathcal L$-theory, let $\phi$ be an $\mathcal L$-sentence, and let $\psi$ be an $\mathcal L$-formula. Then

$$
T\cup\{\phi\}\vdash\psi
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 
T\vdash\phi\rightarrow\psi.
$$

**Definition 2.2.6.** An $\mathcal L$-theory $T$ is *consistent* if there is no $\mathcal L$-formula $\phi$ such that both $T\vdash\phi$ and $T\vdash\neg\phi.$ Otherwise, $T$ is *inconsistent*.

**Proposition 2.2.7 (Finite character of deduction).** Let $T$ be an $\mathcal L$-theory and let $\phi$ be an $\mathcal L$-formula. Then

$$
T\vdash\phi
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 
T_0\vdash\phi\text{ for some finite }T_0\subseteq T.
$$
