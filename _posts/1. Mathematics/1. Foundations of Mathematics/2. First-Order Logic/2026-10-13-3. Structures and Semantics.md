**Definition 2.3.1.** An $\mathcal{L}$-*structure* $\mathcal{M}$ is given by the following data:
1. a nonempty set $M$ called the *underlying set* of $\mathcal{M};$
2. a function $f^{\mathcal{M}}:M^{n_f}\to M$ for each $f\in {\mathcal{F}};$
3. a set $R^{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq M^{n_R}$ for each $R\in {\mathcal{R}};$
4. an element $c^{\mathcal{M}}\in M$ for each $c\in \mathcal{C}.$

The functions $f^{\mathcal M},$ relations $R^{\mathcal M},$ and elements $c^{\mathcal M}$ are the *interpretations* of the symbols $f,R,$ and $c.$

**Definition 2.3.2.** Let $\operatorname{Var}=\{v_1,v_2,\dots\}.$ A *variable assignment* in $\mathcal M$ is a map $s:\operatorname{Var}\to M.$ For $a\in M,$ the assignment $s[x\mapsto a]$ is defined by

$$
s[x\mapsto a](y)=
\begin{cases}
a,&y=x,\\
s(y),&y\ne x.
\end{cases}
$$

**Definition 2.3.3.** The *value* $t^{\mathcal M,s}$ of a term $t$ in $\mathcal M$ under an assignment $s$ is defined recursively by

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^{\mathcal M,s} &= c^{\mathcal M},\\
x^{\mathcal M,s} &= s(x),\\
f(t_1,\dots,t_{n_f})^{\mathcal M,s}
&=f^{\mathcal M}(t_1^{\mathcal M,s},\dots,t_{n_f}^{\mathcal M,s}).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 2.3.4.** The *satisfaction relation* $\mathcal M,s\vDash\phi$ is defined recursively by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M,s\vDash t=u
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t^{\mathcal M,s}=u^{\mathcal M,s},\\
\mathcal M,s\vDash R(t_1,\dots,t_{n_R})
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (t_1^{\mathcal M,s},\dots,t_{n_R}^{\mathcal M,s})\in R^{\mathcal M},\\
\mathcal M,s\vDash\neg\phi
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal M,s\nvDash\phi,\\
\mathcal M,s\vDash\phi\rightarrow\psi
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal M,s\nvDash\phi\text{ or }\mathcal M,s\vDash\psi,\\
\mathcal M,s\vDash(\forall x)\phi
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal M,s[x\mapsto a]\vDash\phi\text{ for every }a\in M.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Proposition 2.3.5.** Let $s$ and $s'$ be assignments in an $\mathcal L$-structure $\mathcal M.$
1. If $s$ and $s'$ agree on $\operatorname{FV}(t),$ then $t^{\mathcal M,s}=t^{\mathcal M,s'}.$
2. If $s$ and $s'$ agree on $\operatorname{FV}(\phi),$ then $\mathcal M,s\vDash\phi$ if and only if $\mathcal M,s'\vDash\phi.$

**Definition 2.3.6.** For an $\mathcal L$-sentence $\sigma,$ we write $\mathcal M\vDash\sigma$ if $\mathcal M,s\vDash\sigma$ for every assignment $s,$ and say that $\sigma$ is *true* in $\mathcal M.$

Let $\phi$ be a formula with free variables among the distinct variables $\bar v=(v_{i_1},\dots,v_{i_m}),$ and let $\bar a=(a_{i_1},\dots,a_{i_m})\in M^m.$ We write $\mathcal M\vDash\phi(\bar a)$ if $\mathcal M,s\vDash\phi$ for an assignment $s$ satisfying $s(v_{i_j})=a_{i_j}$ for $j=1,\dots,m.$

**Lemma 2.3.7 (Substitution lemma).** Let $\mathcal M$ be an $\mathcal L$-structure, let $s$ be an assignment in $\mathcal M,$ and let $t$ be a term.
1. For every term $u,$

   $$
   (u[t/x])^{\mathcal M,s}=u^{\mathcal M,s[x\mapsto t^{\mathcal M,s}]}.
   $$

2. If $t$ is free for $x$ in $\phi,$ then

   $$
   \mathcal M,s\vDash\phi[t/x]
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \mathcal M,s[x\mapsto t^{\mathcal M,s}]\vDash\phi.
   $$

**Definition 2.3.8.** Let $T$ be an $\mathcal L$-theory. An $\mathcal L$-structure $\mathcal M$ is a *model* of $T,$ written $\mathcal M\vDash T,$ if $\mathcal M\vDash\sigma$ for every sentence $\sigma\in T.$ The theory $T$ is *satisfiable* if it has a model. We write

$$
\operatorname{Mod}(T)=\{\mathcal M:\mathcal M\text{ is an }\mathcal L\text{-structure and }\mathcal M\vDash T\}.
$$

**Definition 2.3.9.** Let $T$ be an $\mathcal L$-theory and let $\sigma$ be an $\mathcal L$-sentence. We write $T\vDash\sigma$ and call $\sigma$ a *logical consequence* of $T$ if every model of $T$ satisfies $\sigma.$ A sentence $\sigma$ is *logically valid* if $\varnothing\vDash\sigma,$ in which case we write $\vDash\sigma.$

**Definition 2.3.10.** An $\mathcal L$-structure $\mathcal M$ is a *substructure* of an $\mathcal L$-structure $\mathcal N,$ written $\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal N,$ if
1. $M\subseteq N,$
2. $c^{\mathcal M}=c^{\mathcal N}$ for every constant symbol $c,$
3. $f^{\mathcal M}=f^{\mathcal N}|_{M^{n_f}}$ for every function symbol $f,$ and
4. $R^{\mathcal M}=R^{\mathcal N}\cap M^{n_R}$ for every relation symbol $R.$

**Definition 2.3.11.** Suppose that $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal N$ are $\mathcal L$-structures with underlying sets $M$ and $N,$ respectively. An $\mathcal L$-*homomorphism* $\eta:M\to N$ is a map such that
1. for every constant symbol $c,$

   $$
   \eta(c^{\mathcal M})=c^{\mathcal N};
   $$

2. for every function symbol $f$ and all $a_1,\dots,a_{n_f}\in M,$

   $$
   \eta(f^{\mathcal M}(a_1,\dots,a_{n_f}))
   =f^{\mathcal N}(\eta(a_1),\dots,\eta(a_{n_f}));
   $$

   equivalently, the following diagram commutes: 
   $$
   \begin{tikzcd}
   M^{n_f} \arrow[r, "f^{\mathcal M}"] \arrow[d, "\eta^{n_f}"'] & M \arrow[d, "\eta"] \\
   N^{n_f} \arrow[r, "f^{\mathcal N}"'] & N
   \end{tikzcd}
   $$
   
3. for every relation symbol $R$ and all $a_1,\dots,a_{n_R}\in M,$

   $$
   (a_1,\dots,a_{n_R})\in R^{\mathcal M}
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   (\eta(a_1),\dots,\eta(a_{n_R}))\in R^{\mathcal N}.
   $$

**Definition 2.3.12.** An injective $\mathcal L$-homomorphism $\eta:M\to N$ is an $\mathcal L$-*monomorphism*. An $\mathcal L$-monomorphism is an $\mathcal L$-*embedding* if it both preserves and reflects relations: for every relation symbol $R$ and all $a_1,\dots,a_{n_R}\in M,$

$$
(a_1,\dots,a_{n_R})\in R^{\mathcal M}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\eta(a_1),\dots,\eta(a_{n_R}))\in R^{\mathcal N}.
$$

An embedding identifies $\mathcal M$ with the substructure of $\mathcal N$ carried by $\eta(M).$ A bijective $\mathcal L$-embedding is an $\mathcal L$-*isomorphism*.

**Definition 2.3.13.** Two $\mathcal L$-structures $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal N$ are *elementarily equivalent,* written $\mathcal M\equiv\mathcal N,$ if

$$
\mathcal M\vDash\sigma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal N\vDash\sigma
$$

for every $\mathcal L$-sentence $\sigma.$ The *full theory* of $\mathcal M$ is

$$
\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal M)=\{\sigma:\sigma\text{ is an }\mathcal L\text{-sentence and }\mathcal M\vDash\sigma\}.
$$

**Definition 2.3.14.** A class $\mathcal K$ of $\mathcal L$-structures is an *elementary class* if there is an $\mathcal L$-theory $T$ such that $\mathcal K=\operatorname{Mod}(T).$

**Proposition 2.3.15.** For every $\mathcal L$-structure $\mathcal M,$

$$
\operatorname{Mod}(\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal M))
=\{\mathcal N:\mathcal N\text{ is an }\mathcal L\text{-structure and }\mathcal N\equiv\mathcal M\}.
$$
