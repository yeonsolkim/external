**The interior of a set.** Interior of a set $S$ is a maximal admissible region contained in $S$.

**Definition.** Let $S$ be a subset of a topological space $X$. The *interior* of $S$ is the union of all open sets contained in $S$, denoted by $\operatorname{int}S$.

**Theorem.** Let $S$ be a subset of a topological space $X$, and let $x \in S$. Then $N \subseteq X$ is a neighborhood of $x$ if and only if $x \in \operatorname{int}N$.

*Proof.* If $N \in \mathcal{N}(x)$, then there exists $U \in \mathcal{T}$ such that $x \in U \subseteq N$. It follows that $U \subseteq \operatorname{int}N$, so $x \in U \subseteq \operatorname{int}N$. Conversely, suppose $x \in \operatorname{int}N$. Since $\operatorname{int}N$ is an open set contained in $N$, $N \in \mathcal{N}(x)$. <span class="qed">$\square$</span>

The dual concept of openness is *closedness*.



**Definition.** Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological space. A subset $F$ of $X$ is called a *closed set* if $F^c$ is open.

**Theorem.** 

**Corollary.** Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological space. Then the following hold:
1. $\varnothing$ and $X$ are closed;
2. arbitrary intersections of closed sets are closed;
3. finite unions of closed sets are closed.

**Definition.** Let $S$ be a subset of a topological space $X$. The *closure* of $S$ is the intersection of all closed sets containing $S$, denoted by $\operatorname{cl}S$.

**Proposition.** Let $S$ be a subset of a topological space $X$. Then, $x \in \operatorname{cl}S$ if and only if every open neighborhood of $x$ intersects $S$.
