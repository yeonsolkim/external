**Definition 1.4.1.** Let $k\in\mathbb N_{>0}.$ If $x=(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ and $y=(y_1,\dots,y_k)$ are elements of $\mathbb R^k,$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb R,$ we define the *coordinatewise operations*

$$
x+y=(x_1+y_1,\dots,x_k+y_k),  
\qquad
x-y=(x_1-y_1,\dots,x_k-y_k),  
$$

and

$$
\lambda x = 
(\lambda x_1,\dots,\lambda x_k).  
$$

Then, we define the *inner product* of $x$ and $y$ by 

$$ x\cdot y = \sum_{i=1}^k x_iy_i,$$ 

and the *norm* of $x$ by 

$$ \| x\| = (x\cdot x)^{1/2}.$$ 

We also define the *zero tuple* of $\mathbb R^k$ by

$$  
O=(0,\dots,0).  
$$

<br>

**Proposition 1.4.2.** Let $x,y,z\in \mathbb R^k$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb R.$ Then
1. $0\le \|x\|,$
2. $\|x\|=0 \Leftrightarrow x=\mathit O,$
3. $\|\lambda x\| = |\lambda|\|x\|.$

*Proof.*

<br>

**Theorem 1.4.3.** Let $x,y,z\in \mathbb R^k$ and $\lambda \in \mathbb R.$ Then
1. $|x\cdot y|\le \|x\| \|y\|,$
2. $\|x+y\|\le \|x\|+\|y\|,$
3. $\|x-z\|\le \|x-y\| + \|y-z\|.$
