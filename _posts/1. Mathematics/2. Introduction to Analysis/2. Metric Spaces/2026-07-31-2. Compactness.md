**Definition 2.2.1.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq M.$ A collection $\lbrace U_{i}\rbrace_{i \in I}$ of subsets of $M$ is a *cover* of $A$ if

$$
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i \in I}U_{i}.
$$

A cover of $A$ whose elements are open sets is called an *open cover* of $A$.
A subcollection of a cover of $A$ that still covers $A$ is called a *subcover* of $A$, and a subcover of $A$ whose index set is finite is called a *finite subcover* of $A$.

**Definition 2.2.2.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq E\subseteq M.$ The set $A$ is *compact relative to* $E$ if every cover of $A$ whose elements are open relative to $E$ has a finite subcover of $A$.

**Theorem 2.2.3.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq E\subseteq M.$ Then, $A$ is compact relative to $M$ if and only if $A$ is compact relative to $E.$

*Proof.* Suppose that $A$ is compact relative to $M.$ Let $\lbrace U_{i}\rbrace_{i \in I}$ be a cover of $A$ whose elements are open relative to $E$. By Theorem 2.1.23, there is an open set $V_i$ such that $V_i \cap E=U_i$ for each $i\in I$; and since $A$ is compact relative to $M,$ we have

$$ A\subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^{n}V_{i_k}\tag{\(\ast\)}$$

for some finitely many indices $i_1,\dots,i_n\in I.$ Since $A\subseteq E,$ $(\ast)$ implies

$$ A\subseteq \bigcup_{k=1}^{n}U_{i_k}.\tag{\(\ast\ast\)}$$

Therefore $A$ is compact relative to $E.$
Conversely, suppose that $A$ is compact relative to $E.$ Let $\lbrace V_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ be a collection of open sets which covers $A,$ and let $U_i=V_i\cap E$ for each $i\in I.$ Then $U_i$ is open relative to $E$ for each $i\in I,$ and $\lbrace U_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ covers $A.$ Since $A$ is compact relative to $E,$ $(\ast\ast)$ holds for some finitely many indices $i_1,\dots,i_n\in I.$ Since $U_i\subseteq V_i$ for each $i\in I,$ $(\ast\ast)$ implies $(\ast).$ Therefore $A$ is compact relative to $M.$<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Definition 2.2.4.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq M.$ The set $A$ is *compact* if it is compact relative to $M.$ Equivalently, $A$ is compact if every open cover of $A$ has a finite subcover of $A$.

**Theorem 2.2.5.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq M.$ If $A$ is compact then $A$ is closed.

*Proof.* Suppose $x\in A^c$. For $y\in A$, since $x\ne y$, we have $d(x,y)>0$. Let

$$0< r_y < \frac 1 2 d(x,y).$$

Then the collection $\lbrace B_{r_y} : y\in A\rbrace$ is an open cover of $A$. Since $A$ is compact, there are finitely many points $y_1,\dots,y_n\in A$ such that

$$ A \subseteq \bigcup_{i=1}^n B_{r_i}(y_i) = E.$$

Since $r_{i}<\frac 1 2 d(x,y)$, we have

$$B_{r_i}(y_i) \cap B_{r_i}(x) = \varnothing$$

for each $i=1,\dots,n$. Indeed, if there exists $z\in M$ such that $z\in B_{r_i}(x)$ and $z\in B_{r_i}(y_i)$, then $d(x,y_i)\le d(x,z) + d(z,y_i)<2r_i$, which contradicts $r_{i}<\frac 1 2 d(x,y)$. Therefore, if

$$r = \min \lbrace r_{y_1},\dots,r_{y_n}\rbrace,$$

then $B_r(x)$ does not intersect $E$. Since $A\subseteq E$, we have

$$A \cap B_r(x)=\varnothing,$$

so that $A^c$ is open. By Theorem 2.1.17, $A$ is closed.<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Theorem 2.2.6.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A\subseteq M.$ If $A$ is compact, then every closed subset of $A$ is compact.

*Proof.* Let $E$ be a closed subset of $A$, and $\lbrace U_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ be an open cover of $E$. If $\mathcal A = \lbrace U_i\rbrace_{i\in I} \cup \lbrace E^c\rbrace$, then it is an open cover of $A$. Since $A$ is compact, there is a finite subcollection $\mathcal E$ of $\mathcal A$ which covers $A$. Then $\mathcal E$ covers $E$. Since $\mathcal E \setminus \lbrace E^c\rbrace$ is a finite subcover of $E$, $E$ is compact.<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Corollary 2.2.7.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A,E\subseteq M.$ If $A$ is compact and $E$ is closed, then $A\cap E$ is compact.

*Proof.* Since $A$ is closed by Theorem 2.2.5 and $E$ is closed, $A\cap E$ is closed by (2) of Theorem 2.1.18. Since $A\cap E$ is a closed subset of $A$, Theorem 2.2.6 shows that $A\cap E$ is compact.<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Theorem 2.2.8.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space. If $\lbrace A_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ is a collection of compact subsets of $M$ such that the intersection of every finite subcollection of $\lbrace A_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ is nonempty, then the intersection of $\lbrace A_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ is nonempty.

*Proof.* Let $A_1\in \lbrace A_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$, and suppose, for contradiction, that the intersection of $\lbrace A_i\rbrace_{i\in I}$ is empty. Then, there are no points of $A_1$ that belongs to every $A_i$. Thus there exists $i\in I$ such that $x\in A^{c}_{i}$. Hence the union of the sets $A^{c}_{i}$ is an open cover of $A_1$. Since $A_1$ is compact, there are finitely many indices $i_1,\dots,i_n\in I$ such that

$$ A_1\subseteq A^{c}_{i_1} \cup \cdots \cup A^{c}_{i_n}.$$

It follows that

$$ A_1\cap A^{c}_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap A^{c}_{i_n} = \varnothing,$$

which contradicts our premise.<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Corollary 2.2.9.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space. If $\lbrace a_n\rbrace$ is a sequence of nonempty compact subsets of $M$ such that $a_{n+1}\subseteq a_n$ for $n=1,2,3,\dots$ , then

$$ \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \ne \varnothing.$$

**Theorem 2.2.10.** Let $(M,d)$ be a metric space and let $A$ be a compact subset of $M$. If $E$ is an infinite subset of $A$, then $E$ has a limit point in $A$.

*Proof.* Suppose that no point of $A$ is a limit point of $E$. For each $x\in A$, we have $B_r^{\ast}(x)\cap E = \varnothing$ for some $r>0$. Then $B_r(x)$ contains at most one point of $E$. Thus no finite subcollection of $\lbrace B_{r_x}(x) : x\in A\rbrace$ can cover $E$; the same is true of $A$, since $E\subseteq A$. However, since $\lbrace B_{r_x}(x) : x\in A\rbrace$ is an open cover of $A$, the compactness of $A$ guarantees the existence of a finite subcover of $A$; this is a contradiction.<span class="qed">$\square$</span>

**Theorem 2.2.11.** If $\lbrace I_n \rbrace$ is a sequence of intervals in $\mathbb R$, such that $I_{n+1}\subseteq I_n$ for $n=1,2,3,\dots$ , then

$$ \bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} I_n \ne \varnothing.$$
