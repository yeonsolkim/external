**Definition 3.4.1.** Let $M$ be a metric space and let $(x_n)$ be a sequence in $M$. The sequence $(x_n)$ is called a *Cauchy sequence* if for any $\varepsilon >0$, there exists $n_0\in \mathbb N^{+}$ such that 

$$ m,n\ge n_0\ \Longrightarrow \ d(x_m,x_n)<\varepsilon.$$

**Definition 3.4.2.** Let $A$ be a nonempty subset of a metric space $M$, and let $S$ be the set of all reals of the form $d(x,y)$ where $x,y\in A$. The supremum of $S$ is called the *diameter* of $A$.

**Theorem 3.4.3.** Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence in a metric space $M$. The following are equivalent. 

1. $(x_n)$ converges in $M$.
2. $(x_n)$ is a Cauchy sequence.
3. $\lim\limits_{k\to \infty}\left(\operatorname {diam} {\{x_n\}_{n\ge k}}\right) = 0$.

*Proof.*
