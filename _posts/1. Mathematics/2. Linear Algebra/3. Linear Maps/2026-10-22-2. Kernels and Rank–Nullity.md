To understand the behavior of a linear map, it is useful to examine which vectors in its domain have the same image. For $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$ and $u,v\in V$, linearity gives

$$  
\tau(u)=\tau(v)\iff \tau(u-v)=0_W.  
$$

Thus, the vectors mapped to zero determine precisely which vectors are still identified under the map. This observation motivates the following definition.

**Definition 3.2.1.** Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces, and let $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. The *kernel* of $\tau$ is the set  $\operatorname{ker}\tau$ defined by 

$$ \operatorname{ker}\tau = \{ v\in V : \tau(v)=0_W\}.$$

**Proposition 3.2.2.** Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces, and let $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. 
1. The set $\operatorname{ker}\tau$ is a subspace of $V$.
2. The map $\tau$ is injective if and only if $\operatorname{ker}\tau = \{0_V\}$.

**Definition 3.2.3.** Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces, and let $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. If $\operatorname{ker} \tau$ and $\operatorname{im}\tau$ is finite-dimensional, the dimension of $\operatorname{ker}\tau$ is called the *nullity* of $\tau$, denoted by $\operatorname{null}\tau$, and the dimension of $\operatorname{im}\tau$ is called the *rank* of $\tau$, denoted by $\operatorname{rank}\tau$.

**Theorem 3.2.4 (Rank–nullity theorem).** Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces, and let $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. If $V$ is finite-dimensional, then 

$$ \operatorname{rank}\tau + \operatorname{null}\tau = \dim V.$$

**Theorem 3.2.5.** Let $V$ and $W$ be finite-dimensional vector spaces, and let $\tau\in \operatorname{Hom}(V,W)$. If $\dim V = \dim W$, then the following are equivalent: 
1. the map $\tau$ is injective;
2. the map $\tau$ is surjective;
3. $\operatorname{rank}\tau = \dim V$.
